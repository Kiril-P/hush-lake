# Diagnosis
- The pale, nearly uniform sky leaves the upper half visually empty and gives little sense of time or weather.
- Mountains and repeated tree rows have similar contrast, flattening the distant landscape.
- The ice is uniformly saturated blue, with abrupt bright shards and little surface variation.
- The Eye's broad white bloom overwhelms its black pupil and obscures nearby ice detail.
- The shoreline stalls form a repetitive row with few props or pools of light to suggest habitation.

# Wins, ordered by impact
1. Reduce the Eye's white bloom, darken its pupil, and use a tight aqua emissive rim with a softer falloff across the ice.
2. Grade the sky and ice toward deep teal; layer blue distance fog between shoreline, trees, and mountains.
3. Add small amber lanterns to shoreline structures and localized warm light pools on snow and ice.
4. Break up the ice with subtle faceted color variation and sparse thin crack lines; soften the edges of the raised shards.
5. Place a few low-poly crates, logs, a sled, and a drying rack along the shore to break up the stall silhouettes.

The storybook's painted clouds and intricate reflections, and the Nordic image's volumetric shafts, are illustrative effects; approximate them with simple sky gradients, decals, and restrained fog or light cones. The generated amber sky and extra text should not be copied literally.
