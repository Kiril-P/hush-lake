## Diagnosis
- The pale lavender sky and terrain flatten the cold palette and reduce depth.
- The distant forest and mountains have little atmospheric separation.
- The bright ice tiles overwhelm the small Eye and obscure its black pupil.
- Radial ground facets and repeated trees create visual noise around the lake.
- Sparse, tiny props give the central ice little sense of habitation or scale.

## Wins (highest impact first)
1. Darken the surrounding ice and strengthen the Eye's aqua emissive edge, black pupil and thin amber ring so it reads at aerial distance.
2. Add blue distance fog between forest and mountains; use a restrained cool directional light to separate their silhouettes.
3. Tint ice tiles in a few muted blue values and soften their seams; preserve the central clearing's shape.
4. Place a small hut, lanterns and a few fishing props around the Eye; use the same amber emissive material and small local light pools.
5. Vary tree heights and spacing and add a few low-poly rocks to break the uniform forest silhouette.

The painted fog, intricate ice cracks and volumetric shafts are illustrative targets; approximate them with simple fog, decals and restrained translucent meshes rather than matching every detail.
