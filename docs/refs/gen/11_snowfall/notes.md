## Diagnosis
- The flat lavender sky gives little sense of dusk or weather depth.
- Distant trees and mountains merge into a repetitive, equally sharp band.
- Snowfall is sparse and mostly tiny, so the camp does not feel enveloped by it.
- The ice edge is a stark white stripe; the lake surface reads as a large uniform blue plane.
- The bright central crystal competes with the cropped Eye, while the camp lacks small lived-in props.

## Wins (ordered by impact)
1. Reduce crystal emission and preserve a dark pupil and defined aqua ring on the Eye; keep its glow local.
2. Add blue distance fog in layers, fading mountains and far trees while keeping camp silhouettes readable.
3. Use layered snow particles with varied sizes and speeds, including a few soft foreground flakes.
4. Add amber point lights at the hut and stalls, with restrained warm pools on adjacent snow.
5. Break up the ice with low-poly floes, subtle cracks and snow-covered camp props.

Painterly brushwork and perfectly volumetric shafts are illustrative; approximate them with simple gradients, fog and restrained light cones.
