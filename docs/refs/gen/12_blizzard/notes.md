## Diagnosis
- The pale lavender sky and broad vertical light band flatten the blizzard's depth.
- Distant trees and stalls remain uniformly visible, so the long view is not convincingly lost.
- Large bright snow polygons compete with the Eye instead of conveying wind.
- The ice is an even blue field; its shards lack contact snow and material contrast.
- Stalls have sparse warm light and little lived-in detail, leaving the horizon visually inert.

## Wins, ordered by impact
1. Darken the sky to desaturated teal and increase distance fog so trees dissolve behind the stalls.
2. Use wind-driven, varied-size snow particles and low drifting snow layers; keep the Eye's dark pupil and aqua rim legible.
3. Add small amber stall lamps with restrained pools of light and soft ice reflections.
4. Add snow caps to stall roofs and shard bases, plus sparse crates, lanterns and fishing props near the stalls.
5. Add subtle ice cracks and a broad, soft aqua glow around the Eye; grade down the foreground blue.

The painted ice reflections, intricate drifting snow and volumetric shafts are illustrative targets; approximate them with simple shaders, particles and translucent geometry rather than expecting exact reproduction.
